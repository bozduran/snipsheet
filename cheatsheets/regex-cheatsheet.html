Regex glossary

the words people throw around, in plain language
literal
A character that matches itself — most letters and digits. The letter a matches an a.
metacharacter
A character with special meaning rather than its literal one: . * + ? ^ $ ( ) [ ] { } | \\.
character class
A set of characters that may appear in one position, like [a-z] or the shorthand \\d.
quantifier
How many times the preceding item repeats: * + ? or {n,m}.
greedy vs lazy
Greedy quantifiers grab as much as possible; adding ? makes them lazy, grabbing as little as possible.
anchor
Matches a position, not a character — ^, $, \\b. Consumes nothing.
assertion
A zero-width test that something is (or isn't) true at this spot. Anchors and lookarounds are assertions.
group
Parentheses that bundle part of a pattern, optionally capturing what it matched.
capturing group
A group whose match is saved and numbered, reusable as \\1 / $1.
non-capturing group
(?:…) — groups without saving the match.
backreference
\\1 or \\k<name> — match the same text a previous group captured.
alternation
The | operator, meaning “either this or that”.
lookahead / lookbehind
Assert what does (or doesn't) come after / before, without consuming it.
flag / modifier
Options that change matching: i ignore case, g global, m multiline, s dotall.
escape
A backslash that strips a metacharacter's power so it matches literally: \\. matches a real dot.
word boundary
\\b — the edge between a word character and a non-word character.
dotall (single-line)
The s flag: . also matches newline characters.
multiline
The m flag: ^ and $ match at every line break, not just the ends.
named group
A capturing group given a name instead of (or as well as) a number.
catastrophic backtracking
A pattern with nested, overlapping quantifiers that can take exponential time on some inputs. Avoid (a+)+-style nesting.
